Superconducting microwave resonators are essential for hybrid quantum systems and low-loss signal transduction platforms.  
In this project, we designed and fabricated **niobium nitride (NbN)** coplanar waveguide (CPW) resonators on sapphire substrates, integrated with **yttrium iron garnet (YIG)** spheres to demonstrate strong **magnon–photon coupling** at cryogenic temperatures.

---

### Fabrication and Design

The resonators were patterned using **electron-beam lithography (EBL)** and **reactive ion etching (RIE)** from high-quality NbN films deposited via **DC magnetron sputtering**.  
Each resonator was optimized for high internal quality factor and efficient coupling to the YIG sphere.

### Measurement and Characterization

The coupled system was measured in a **cryogenic probe station** and later in a **dilution refrigerator** for low-temperature studies.  
The transmission response (S<sub>21</sub>) revealed **avoided level crossing**, confirming the **strong coupling regime** between resonator photons and YIG magnons.

### Results and Outlook

We observed a **coupling strength (g/2π) exceeding the linewidths** of both the resonator and magnon modes, confirming the system’s entry into the **strong coupling regime**.  
These results pave the way for hybrid quantum architectures integrating **superconducting circuits and spin systems**.

> _“Hybrid systems like NbN–YIG platforms bridge the gap between superconducting quantum circuits and magnonic information carriers.”_

---

### Related Work

This effort is part of ongoing research into **quantum-compatible magnonic devices**, supported through collaborative work on cryogenic microwave characterization and hybrid spin-photon integration.
